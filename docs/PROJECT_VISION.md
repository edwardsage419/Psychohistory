# Project Vision

## North star

Build a long running independent evidence layer for machine forecasting.

Psychohistory should preserve what information was available, what method produced a forecast, what probability was issued, how the outcome was resolved, how the forecast scored, and why the method failed when evidence supports a failure classification.

The system should become more valuable as genuine prospective history accumulates. Historical continuity, falsifiability, provenance, calibration, and failure knowledge matter more than short term novelty or model scale.

## Strategic position

Psychohistory should answer a durable question:

Which forecasts deserve trust, based on what was actually known, issued, resolved, and measured at the time?

The project should remain model neutral. Statistical baselines, econometric models, language models, agentic systems, expert estimates, public consensus estimates, and market implied probabilities may all become subjects of the same evidence and evaluation framework when legally and methodologically appropriate.

## Primary users

The initial user is the project owner.

The architecture should support later public researchers, forecasting teams, risk functions, model developers, investment research groups, and institutions that need independent forecast validation without paying the complexity cost of an early multi user platform.

## Long term moat

The project should deliberately accumulate assets that are difficult to recreate later:

1. prospective forecast records created before outcomes are known
2. immutable point in time evidence and method lineage
3. stable versioned contracts across many years
4. explicit failed, ambiguous, unresolved, and corrected records
5. calibration and baseline comparison history
6. a structured Forecast Failure Corpus
7. comparable trust profiles across changing model generations
8. operational continuity under near zero recurring cost

Code can be copied. Elapsed prospective history cannot be reconstructed after the fact.

## Core program

### Forecast Trust Core

Provide machine readable contracts and deterministic verification for:

1. target definitions
2. resolution rules
3. point in time evidence and feature snapshots
4. forecast methods and fitted state where relevant
5. run attempts
6. forecast issuance
7. append only corrections
8. outcome resolution
9. frozen evaluation cohorts
10. scoring and calibration outputs

### Prospective Forecast Ledger

Create a small curated stream of genuine prospective forecasts only after the minimum Trust Core is accepted.

Every formal issuance must have a defensible target, horizon, information cutoff, evidence snapshot, method identity, resolution rule, and immutable content identity.

Question quality and provenance depth take priority over volume.

### Forecast Failure Corpus

As outcomes mature, preserve structured evidence about forecast error and failure modes.

The corpus should distinguish, when evidence permits, data failures, information boundary failures, method failures, calibration failures, regime changes, semantic failures, resolution problems, and unresolved causal attribution.

### Trust and Audit Layer

Use the accumulated ledger to evaluate forecasting methods under common contracts.

The system should make it possible to inspect historical calibration, horizon sensitivity, event class performance, baseline relative value, stability across regimes, and evidence quality.

### Public and Commercial Interfaces

Productization should follow accumulated evidence.

Preferred sequence:

1. static public ledger views and exports
2. machine readable verification and research datasets
3. professional comparison reports
4. private institutional forecast audits and custom benchmarks
5. paid API or hosted services when demonstrated demand justifies recurring cost

A small number of high value institutional relationships is a better operating fit than a support intensive mass consumer product.

## Supporting measurement research

Psychohistory may continue to convert public observations into reproducible indicators and state representations when they support a concrete forecast target, trust evaluation, or research question.

Each measurement candidate keeps its own evidence gates. Failure or restriction of one candidate does not block unrelated forecast work with independent evidence foundations.

GKG remains an experimental media attention candidate and a preserved case study in measurement validity, evidence recovery, provenance, and semantic uncertainty.

## Forecast scope

The project may eventually support:

1. binary event forecasts with explicit resolution
2. directional or categorical trend forecasts with predefined boundaries
3. continuous probabilistic forecasts when point in time measurement and scoring semantics are mature

Formal forecasts should remain distinct from scenarios, indicator values, narratives, and decisions.

## Product boundaries

Psychohistory must communicate uncertainty and preserve the distinction between observation, inference, forecast, resolution, evaluation, and decision support.

Prior forecasts remain immutable in substance after issuance. Corrections append history.

Unresolved outcomes stay unresolved when the accepted rule cannot support a defensible result.

Historical experiments must declare whether they are genuine contemporaneous forecasts, faithful historical replays, or retrospective model experiments.

No current model should receive contemporaneous historical credit merely because explicit prompt context was restricted to an old date.

## What the project should avoid

The project should avoid resource intensive competition in:

1. frontier model training
2. large forecasting communities
3. prediction market liquidity
4. high frequency market prediction
5. broad real time news aggregation
6. feature volume
7. early enterprise infrastructure
8. opaque global risk scoring

These areas consume resources without strengthening the intended moat.

## Success criteria

The project becomes meaningfully successful when it can demonstrate over a sustained period:

1. a verifier can authenticate formal forecast lineage and detect substantive mutation
2. genuine prospective forecasts accumulate under stable point in time rules
3. outcome resolution is reproducible and independently constrained
4. scoring and calibration preserve complete denominators and baseline comparisons
5. failed and unresolved records remain visible
6. the Failure Corpus supports repeatable analysis of recurring failure modes
7. multiple forecast methods can be compared under common evidence rules
8. public research outputs remain useful with low maintenance burden
9. an institutional user can obtain decision relevant forecast audit evidence that is difficult to recreate from ordinary model outputs
10. the system remains financially sustainable for one operator

## Cost strategy

Default to zero or near zero recurring cost.

Use local deterministic execution, public data with suitable terms, compact versioned artifacts, Git based provenance, and lightweight analytical storage when needed.

Introduce paid services only when they materially improve reliability, reproducibility, product value, or revenue capacity enough to justify recurring cost.

## Time horizon

Optimize for ten to twenty years of continuity.

A modest system with a decade of trustworthy prospective records can hold more durable value than a sophisticated interface with weak historical evidence.
