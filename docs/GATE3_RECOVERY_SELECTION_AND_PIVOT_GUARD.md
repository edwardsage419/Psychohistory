# Gate 3 Recovery, Selection-Bias and Pivot Guard

Date: 2026-09-07
Status: current scientific-governance safeguard. This document does not authorize network recovery, semantic review, source integration, indicator promotion, composite construction, forecasting, calibration, or backtesting.

## Purpose

Prevent two failure modes from distorting Psychohistory while the GKG candidate remains in Gate 3:

1. treating recovered/reviewable historical articles as though they were an unbiased sample of all frozen token-positive cases
2. allowing evidence recovery for one candidate source family to become an open-ended project objective

The project north star remains heterogeneous public observations, transparent indicators, dated probabilistic forecasts, independent outcome resolution, and empirical evaluation. GKG is one experimental media-attention candidate within that larger system.

## Basis in accepted evidence

Phase 5 already established that historical context availability is strongly uneven across years and explicitly warned that survivor-only semantic comparisons would be biased. It also recorded that the English cue extractor and compact quote budget affect which documents become reviewable.

The frozen sample is valuable because failures, unavailable cases, insufficient context and identity conflicts remain visible. Recovery can improve the amount of objective evidence, but successful recovery does not make the recovered subset random or representative.

Evidence-sufficiency version `1.0.1` correctly distinguishes identity-confirmed but context-insufficient E1 from review-ready E2/E3. That correction protects context quality; it does not by itself solve informative missingness or recovery-selection bias.

## Guard A: readiness is an evidence-volume threshold, not a representativeness claim

The current readiness rule remains:

* at least 24 review-ready E2/E3 contexts per token
* at least 4 review-ready E2/E3 contexts per allocated year

Meeting those counts may authorize the next bounded independent semantic-review stage. It does not establish:

* population classifier precision
* recall
* random missingness
* historical representativeness of the review-ready subset
* absence of source survivorship
* absence of cue/extractor selection effects

No document may describe the readiness threshold as proof of those properties.

## Guard B: selection and missingness audit before semantic promotion

Before any audited GKG token can enter Gate 5 experimental historical-indicator promotion, Gate 3B/4 analysis must explicitly assess whether reviewability and successful recovery are associated with variables that could bias semantic conclusions.

At minimum, preserve and compare by token and allocated year:

* frozen selected count
* review-ready E2/E3 count
* E1 count
* identity probable, mismatch and unresolved counts
* human identity decision where present
* original source/outlet
* frozen selection role where available
* recovery method and recovery outcome
* whether context came from original/same-publisher or archive evidence

Where sample size permits, also inspect whether reviewability differs materially by source/outlet, cohort, historical age and frozen selection role.

The audit must keep unavailable and insufficient cases in the denominator accounting. A case does not disappear because semantic review cannot be completed.

## Guard C: sensitivity to unresolved cases

After independent human semantic labels exist, report semantic-match rates with their actual reviewed denominator and the full frozen selected denominator visible side by side.

If unresolved or unreviewable cases are numerous enough that plausible assignments to those cases could materially change a promotion conclusion, the conclusion remains limited or unresolved. A favorable reviewed-subset rate alone is insufficient.

A future analysis may use explicit worst-case/best-case or other preregistered missingness-sensitivity bounds. Do not invent a favorable imputation rule after seeing labels.

Recall remains `recall_not_estimated` unless a separately valid relevant-document frame is created. Positive-only frozen sampling cannot estimate recall by itself.

## Guard D: cue-conditioned context is not semantic ground truth

The frozen Phase 5 cue vocabulary locates compact text. It does not assign the semantic label.

A cue-containing context can still be incidental, ambiguous or a semantic mismatch. Independent humans must retain all allowed semantic labels when review begins.

A missing cue does not establish semantic irrelevance. E1 or unresolved status records an evidence limitation, not a negative semantic judgment.

Any future change to cue/extractor semantics that can alter evidence sufficiency requires an explicit new version and trust binding. It may not silently reinterpret the current frozen evidence.

## Guard E: GKG recovery is bounded candidate work

GKG is a candidate media-attention observation family. The project is not required to rescue GKG to production status.

After the deterministic recovery manifest and after each separately authorized bounded network-recovery batch, governance must reassess recovery viability before authorizing another batch.

Permitted governance outcomes are:

1. continue another bounded recovery batch because objective evidence gain still justifies it
2. restrict the candidate to a narrower historical period or use case
3. pause the candidate pending a genuinely new recovery method or source capability
4. reject the candidate for historical-indicator promotion while preserving all results as scientific evidence

No fixed numeric recovery-yield threshold is introduced here because current evidence does not justify one. The decision must disclose recovered evidence gain, remaining deficit, failure composition, expected additional information, cost/complexity and whether another batch can change the scientific decision.

Missing evidence never justifies lowering identity, context, reviewer or semantic thresholds.

## Guard F: candidate gates are local; the project is not globally locked to GKG

Gates 1 through 5 should be interpreted at the relevant source/measurement candidate level unless a document explicitly defines a project-wide prerequisite.

Failure or restriction of the GKG candidate does not invalidate Psychohistory and does not permanently block research on other source families.

A new source family may begin a separately authorized Gate 1/2 admission and measurement-validation path when governance selects it, even if GKG remains limited or rejected. Such work is not authorized merely by this document.

Gate 6B multi-source state construction still requires multiple sufficiently validated and sufficiently independent indicators. Starting another source candidate is different from claiming multi-source state readiness.

## Goal-alignment check

Before authorizing a substantial new task, ask:

* Does the task improve evidence integrity, measurement validity, source diversity, forecast falsifiability, evaluation integrity, or useful decision support?
* Is the task required by the current gate or a clearly bounded candidate lifecycle?
* Would success change a scientific or product decision?
* Is the project spending effort to preserve an old implementation merely because it already exists?
* Could a simpler rejection, restriction or pivot preserve more progress toward the north star?

If a task cannot answer these questions convincingly, it should not become the next accepted task.

## Current consequence

The current deterministic recovery-manifest task remains valid and remains the only authorized advancement task.

The manifest is useful because it makes the remaining GKG evidence problem explicit, bounded and auditable. It does not commit the project to indefinite recovery.

Reaching the current E2/E3 readiness threshold can permit a later independent semantic-review task, but Gate 5 promotion remains blocked until selection/missingness risk and historical semantic stability are explicitly addressed.
